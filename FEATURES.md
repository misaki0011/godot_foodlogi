# Fresh Routes — Features

Extracted from `SPEC.md`. Status reflects actual implementation state, not spec intent — update both files together when scope changes.

Status legend: **Done** (built and verified) · **In Progress** · **Planned** (MVP scope, not started) · **Backlog** (explicitly post-MVP per §13/§14) · **Retired** (built once, since removed from the game).

The Godot build is a 3D-isometric port of `fresh-routes-mvp.html`, the reference prototype. Where the HTML differs from SPEC.md's exact wording, the port follows the HTML (see the note after the table).

| TERR-01 | Terrain rendering | Done | P0 | CORE-07 | Only two terrain kinds exist today, matching the HTML MVP: open Plains and a single River column. Rendered via GridMap with Python-generated (vertex-colored, self-contained) block meshes. Forest/Mountain/Snow were full-game-only (§8) and out of MVP scope; TERR-06 brings them in as the roster's authoring vocabulary (STAGE-01), which needs a block mesh per new terrain kind. **v0.7 item 67 made the board a bar of chocolate**: a tile is a **flat plate** — a light coloured top face over a chamfered darker rim over a full-footprint skirt — replacing the chunky cube with a grass cap, four corner tufts and a six-unit plinth. Route tiles (ROUTE-06) are built the same way, so a road and the ground it crosses read as the same object. **The rim is the grid line and has to be colour, not shading**: shadows are desktop-only (LOOP-08), and a groove that exists only as a shadow leaves the whole board one flat green sheet on web and mobile. The skirt is structural for a related reason — the body is only its full width across a band at its middle, so with nothing full-footprint beneath it the groove between two tiles is a hole the camera sees straight through. Only the *bottom* of a tile moved: the top face still lands on `map_to_local(cell) + (0, 1, 0)`, the surface markers, road slabs, bridge decks, the route overlay and the click-to-cell plane are all built around. | §12, §8.1, §8.5, §0.67 |
| TERR-09 | Ground vegetation | Done | P2 | TERR-01 | Detail moves from *inside* every tile to *on* a minority of them: a tree or a bush stands on roughly a third of the free plains cells, from four Python-generated props (broad round-topped tree, taller slim tree, and two bushes) placed by `TerrainRenderer` with a small random yaw, size and offset. **A bush is a rounded block sitting straight on the grass** -- one square, one stretched into a rectangle -- with a rosette of leaves sprouting out of the top and nothing underneath it; an earlier version stood one in a planter, which put a piece of furniture on ground the player builds roads over. The rectangle's leaves gather over one END rather than its middle, since a long block with a centred rosette reads as a wide shrub while an off-centre cluster reads as a hedge, and that is what tells the two shapes apart at map distance. Two geometry constraints are load-bearing: corner rounding is a fixed *fraction* of the block's smallest side (and cannot pass ~a sixth, or the three-box chamfer degenerates into a plus sign), and the body is pitched more saturated than `GRASS_TOP` and lighter than a tree canopy, since a green picked halfway between the two comes out the tile's own colour and vanishes into it. Three rules keep it from fighting the game. **The scatter is a pure function of the cell coordinates**, not a seeded RNG — `_render_grid` rebuilds the map on every hover, day and build, so anything re-rolled per render makes the forest crawl; the same map always grows the same trees in the same places. **A node gets a one-cell clearing** (its footprint plus the eight cells around it), since a prop is tall enough to cut across a node's order chips from the fixed camera angle. **Anything built clears the ground and bulldozing grows it back**: the same render hides props on cells in `GameState.grid` and shows the rest, so a drag, a sweep, an upgrade and a bulldoze are all correct without any of them knowing about it. `verify_main._test_ground_vegetation` asserts the scatter dresses open ground without carpeting it, that no prop sits on a node or its clearing or in the river, that a re-render places the identical set of props, and that a prop hides under a tile built on its cell and returns when that tile is cleared. | §8.5, §0.67 |

| ROUTE-05 | Route upgrade/bulldoze | Done | P1 | ROUTE-01, ROUTE-04 | A route tile can be upgraded Dirt→Paved→Main for a flat cost; bulldozing any built tile removes it with **no refund**, matching the HTML (this is a deviation from the old 50%-refund behavior). A bridge tile keeps its own `level` throughout; hub and storage cells replace the route cell outright, so `Main._do_build_hub`/`_do_build_storage` record the covered level on the cell for `_clear_cell` to restore — clearing the road too would swallow infrastructure the player never asked to lose and split the network in half. | ROUTE-17 | Bridge: cross a route without joining it | Done | P2 | ROUTE-12, HUB-05 | A **placed structure**, not a drag-time effect: the Bridge tool (§60) converts one existing route tile carrying a straight through-run of road into a road-over-road crossing, the single paid exception to ROUTE-12's interior-must-be-empty-ground rule. | BUILD-01 | Undo the last build action | Done | P1 | ROUTE-01, ROUTE-05, ROUTE-15, STOR-01, HUB-01, DEV-03 | Every build action was final and none of them refunded (ROUTE-05), so a road dragged one tile wide of where it was meant to go cost §8 a tile to place and a second gesture to clear, with nothing handed back either way. **Undo** (`Main._undo_last`, one full-width button under the build tools, plus Ctrl+Z) takes the last action back whole -- tiles, connections, treasury and a source's `upgrade_level` alike -- across all eight build actions: route drag, bulldoze, bulldoze/upgrade sweep, route upgrade, storage, hub, bridge and source expansion, to a depth of 30. **Snapshots, not a per-tool inverse.** Those eight actions compose -- a sweep is n bulldozes, a bulldoze hands the road back at whatever level it was rather than at dirt, a bridge drops two deck connections and leaves the road beneath, a hub remembers the paving it covered -- so an inverse per tool is eight chances to hand back the wrong thing, and the wrong thing here is money. `_push_undo` records `grid` + `connections` + `balance` + every source's expansion level (`duplicate(true)`, since both dictionaries are one level deep in more dictionaries and a shallow copy would leave those shared), which cannot disagree with what the action did because it never has to know what that was. Cheap for the same reason the map is small: region 1 is 21x14, so a whole grid is at most 294 tiny dictionaries. **The rollover clears the history, and that rule is what keeps it honest**: the day pays out against the network as it stood, so undoing a road after it has carried a delivery would refund its cost while the player keeps the income it earned -- repeatable every day with the same road. Undo reaches back to the start of the current day and no further, which is also why it never touches `active_orders`, `filled_lines` or any latched score. **A refused action records nothing** -- every `_push_undo` sits after its action's guards and before its first mutation, so a build stopped for money, for the wrong tile or for a cap leaves the history alone; otherwise Undo's first press would be spent undoing nothing. The button is **disabled** when the history is empty rather than live and apologetic, and its tooltip names what the next press would take back. **Full width, outside the two-column tool grid**: everything in that grid selects a *mode* the map then waits for, and a ninth cell would have read as a tenth tool. Ctrl+Z is desktop convenience only -- the game is playable from a phone browser and nothing may depend on a keyboard (UI-03). One-shot removal ghosts on the Effects layer are freed on undo (a swept one can have up to `TILE_POP_STAGGER_BUDGET` of stagger still to run, so the tile would come back underneath its own disappearance); the payout labels sharing that layer are left alone, since they report a day the undo has no claim on. `verify_main._test_undo` builds and takes back one of each shape -- a drag, a hub over a **paved** tile, a bulldoze, a source expansion -- asserting grid, connections and treasury land exactly where they started, that a refused build records no step, and that a rollover empties the history and greys the button. | §10.9, §0.73 |

A road stays nearer 1.0 on XZ because at rest its tile is flush with its neighbours, so the splat overlaps them; a structure has clear space and can splat harder. **Fast in, slower out**: `TRANS_QUINT` front-loads the travel into an 0.11s rise against a 0.20s settle, and the settle's `TRANS_BACK` undershoots past 1.0 on the way down, which is the secondary squash for free. Stagger tightens to 0.035s on a 0.4s budget so a ten-tile drag still finishes in 0.66s. **Removal is the punch backwards but not symmetric** -- a 0.07s anticipation stretch, then a 0.16s crush that **spreads** to `(1.3, 0.02, 1.3)`, since a uniform shrink to nothing reads as the tile flying away from the camera rather than being demolished. **Two constructions item 64 left out now land too**: a **route upgrade** swaps the block outright (Dirt, Paved and Main are three meshes), staggering along a swept upgrade like a drag; and an **expanded source** (DEV-03), the one construction that is not a grid cell -- its markers live in `NodeMarkers`, which `_render_grid` never rebuilds, so they are popped **directly** rather than through `_pending_tile_pops`, with nothing to arm or clear, and a multi-cell source lands all its markers **together** since they are one building. The check adds that a landing structure starts flat *and spread*, that an upgraded tile pops exactly once, and that a source's markers survive a later render; it restores the treasury and un-expands the source it borrows, since the DEV-03 check further down expects an untouched map. | §10.1, §0.64, §0.65 |

## Known deviation from SPEC.md v0.3 text

SPEC.md v0.3 §0/§4.1/§4.4 describes **atomic** hub-forming route placement: if a new route tile would require a Small Hub the player can't afford, or the network is already at its hub cap, the whole placement (route + hub) should be rejected with no map change and no charge. `fresh-routes-mvp.html` — the reference this port originally followed — instead implemented the earlier v0.2 behavior: the route tile is placed and charged immediately, and a separate `checkAutoHubs` pass afterward either auto-builds the hub, marks the tile `needsHub` (funds too low), or marks it `hub_capped` (network at cap). This port has moved past both of those: hub construction is a separate, manual, player-paid action via the Build Hub tool, buildable on any existing route tile subject only to the per-network cap (HUB-01, HUB-06; v0.5 item 17) — see SPEC.md §0 changelog and "Manual hub construction on any route tile" under §4.4. Neither the HTML's auto-build nor SPEC.md's atomic-rejection describes current behavior; both are kept in their respective docs as historical/aspirational context only.

## Known deviation from fresh-routes-mvp.html's node positions

The HTML places Farm at (3,4) and Bakery at (3,9), only 2-3 tiles (Chebyshev distance) from Garden at (2,6), and both sit close enough to the grid edge that a wide supply bubble could run off-map. With TERR-03's always-on supply speech bubbles, sources that close together (or that close to the edge) crowded or clipped their bubbles. This port moves Farm to (3,2) and Bakery to (3,11) so every source is at least 4 tiles from every other source and at least 2 tiles from the grid's outer edge (valid range x:[2,18], y:[2,11] on the 21x14 grid). Harbor also moved one tile, from (17,9) to (18,9): once bubbles doubled in size, Harbor's supply bubble started overlapping City E's demand bubbles on screen (only 2 tiles apart) even though no source-spacing rule was violated -- source-to-settlement distance was never constrained before. Garden and Dairy keep their original HTML coordinates. Supply amounts, food mappings, and all other node positions are unchanged.
